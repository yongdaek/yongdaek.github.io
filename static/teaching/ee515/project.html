Project Information

  • Introduction. This course requires a group or individual project. Each project should have some "research" aspect (attack, analysis, or measurement) — your aim should be to learn something we couldn't learn just from reading a few papers. For example, you can implement an attack on a small-scale system, or measure a security-relevant large-scale phenomenon that hasn't been measured before.
  • Group size. 1 to 5 members. You have to find your own group members.
  • Important dates
    • Pre-proposal: 9/27/2026, 11:59 PM
    • Full Proposal: 10/11/2026, 11:59 PM
    • Midterm Report: 11/8/2026, 11:59 PM
    • Final Report: 12/16/2026, 11:59 PM (NO EXTENSION!!)

Deliverables

  • Pre-proposal (0%, but required). One-page pre-proposal ("preproposal.pdf") describing Who (members + project name), What (the problem), Why (why it matters), How (relevant papers/tools), and work distribution. Feedback will be given, possibly suggesting a different direction.
  • Full Proposal. Up to three pages (10pt, two-column), incorporating the feedback: Who / What / Why (detailed motivation & related work) / How (approach, data, evaluation) / Management plan (who does what, with a realistic timetable and milestone) / work distribution.
  • Midterm Report. A tar-gzipped directory with any deliverables (e.g., source code) and at least a 6-page report ("report.pdf", 10pt, two-column), organized following the final-report guideline. Note what extra work you'll do if ahead, what difficulties you hit if behind, and what you'd drop if necessary, plus a per-member summary of work and hours.
  • Presentation. Each group gives an in-class presentation of their work. See the evaluation guideline below.
  • Final Report. A tar-gzipped directory with all deliverables and a 10–12 page report ("report.pdf", 10pt, two-column) in the style of a scientific paper: motivation, approach, results, contribution, related work, and conclusions. A separate "contrib.pdf" summarizes the hours spent and contributions of each member.

Notes

  • To get full credit, the project should contain new ideas (new attack, analysis, implementation, or measurement) that could be publishable in an academic conference (not necessarily top-tier).
  • A simple re-implementation without new analysis or comparison will not get full credit.
  • A simple summary of papers will not get full credit either.

Presentation Evaluation Guideline

(Delivery / Presentation adapted from Nick Hopper's guideline.)

Delivery — Mechanics of Communication (20%)

  • Effective use of visual aids
  • Audible from every seat
  • Avoided distracting movement and habits

Presentation — Engagement (20%)

  • Well-organized; good time management (15 ± 1 min)
  • Actively drew the audience in (eye contact, vocal/facial variation, humor, questions)
  • Attentive to the audience's needs (anticipating misunderstandings, readability)

Content (40%)

  • Clear intro: what the talk is about
  • Conveyed the paper's core clearly (problem definition, challenge, approach)
  • Conclusion summarized the key ideas and results
  • Mapped the research lineage — work before & after this paper

Q&A Defense (20%)

  • Answered the Best Question verbally in class with depth
  • Handled follow-ups and pushback; showed real command of the paper
