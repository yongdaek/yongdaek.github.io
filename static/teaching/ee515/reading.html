Reading Report (Precise and Concise)

  • Due: 24 hours before the class in which the paper is discussed (late submission will be regarded as 0).
  • Format: pure text (no html, pdf, word, or hwp). Start with the paper title, your name, and student ID.
  • You may freely use LLMs. The real evaluation happens in the in-class Best Question (BQ) discussion, so read the paper for yourself.

Part 1 — Submit before class

  • Summary (30 pts). Concisely summarize the target system/service, vulnerability, exploitation (attacks), evaluation/experimental method, and defense.
  • Core Causality (10 pts). Identify the one essential cause the paper reveals beyond what was already known. The author may not state it explicitly. Argue why it is the heart of the paper, not a side result.
  • Weak Point (10 pts). Point out the weakest link in the paper's threat model or key experiment. Cite concrete numbers or conditions from the paper.
  • Lineage (10 pts). Find two follow-up papers. One inherits the core idea of this paper; the other borrows only the surface result and goes elsewhere. Classify which is which and justify, in light of the core causality you identified.
  • Open Problem (10 pts). Is the problem this paper raised solved today? If not, why — is it a technical limitation, or a structural/incentive issue?
  • Questions to the presenter (10 pts). Raise questions that arose while writing the above. The Best Question (BQ) will be selected (by the presenter together with the instructor).

In class

  • 15-minute presentation, followed by the BQ discussion: the student who raised the Best Question leads the Q&A with the presenter; the instructor steps in when answers fall short. Answers are given verbally in class (not written in advance).

Part 2 — Submit for the next class

  • Summary of the BQ Q&A (20 pts). Summarize the in-class Best Question discussion — what was asked, how the presenter responded, what the instructor added, and whether it was resolved or remains open.
